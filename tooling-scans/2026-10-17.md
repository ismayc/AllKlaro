# Monthly tooling scan: 2026-10-17

Second scan in this series. The first is `tooling-scans/2026-09-19.md`, and
this report follows up on its WATCH items, checks whether its ADOPT items
actually landed, and corrects one thing it got wrong.

Run in an ephemeral Linux cloud sandbox with no Apple Silicon, no MLX, no
Ollama, no GPU, and none of the private recordings in `~/.cache/allklaro`.
That split decides what could be measured here and what is handed off.

## Summary

Four things are worth acting on, and one of them is a genuinely better
option rather than dependency drift.

1. **Gemma 4 exists, and last month's report said it did not.** This is the
   correction. The 2026-09-19 scan stated "No Gemma 4 exists; the Ollama
   library still lists Gemma 3 at 270M, 1B, 4B, 12B, and 27B as current."
   That was wrong. Gemma 4 launched on 2026-04-02, and `gemma4:12b`, the
   direct same-tier counterpart to the incumbent `gemma3:12b`, was announced
   on 2026-06-03. It is in the Ollama library now at 7.7 GB with a 256K
   context. It is also **Apache-2.0**, where Gemma 3 shipped under the
   source-available Gemma Terms of Use, so the candidate is more freely
   licensed than the incumbent, not less. **NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK, highest
   priority**, and it is the one item on this list that could move the thing
   the app is actually bottlenecked on.

2. **The serving stack upgrade recommended last month never landed, and the
   gap has widened.** `pyproject.toml` and `uv.lock` still carry FastAPI
   0.139.2, uvicorn 0.51.0, and Starlette 1.3.1. Current upstream is FastAPI
   0.143.3, uvicorn 0.54.0, and Starlette 1.8.0. The repo's own suite passes
   identically on both: **767 passed, 35 skipped** either way. **ADOPT.**
   Note that FastAPI 0.139.2 declares only `starlette>=0.46.0` with no upper
   bound, so the lockfile is the sole thing holding Starlette at 1.3.1. Any
   lock refresh crosses to 1.8.0 whether or not the upgrade is chosen
   deliberately, which makes testing it the cheap option rather than the
   thorough one.

3. **`httpx` has been handed off to new maintainers as `httpx2`, and the
   migration is mechanical for this project.** The incumbent `httpx` 0.28.1
   was last released 2024-12-06. `httpx2` 2.13.1 (BSD-3-Clause, 2026-09-23)
   is the continuation, now stewarded by Pydantic Services Inc. with Tom
   Christie still credited as author. AllKlaro's entire `httpx` surface is
   five files and one import line each. Swapping them in a scratch copy of
   the repo gave **764 passed, 36 skipped**, byte-identical to the `httpx`
   arm. **ADOPT**, with the live-Ollama leg confirmed on the Mac first.

4. **`build_noun_forms.py` fetches a lexicon from a moving branch**, which
   is the exact issue the project just fixed for Silero in `b97e92c`.
   `CSV_URL` points at `raw.githubusercontent.com/gambolputty/german-nouns/
   main/german_nouns/nouns.csv`, with no revision pin and no checksum.
   **ADOPT a pin**, using a commit SHA since that repository publishes no
   tags.

**NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK**, in priority order:

- `gemma4:12b` against `gemma3:12b` on the 22-probe integration suite.
- Ollama's MLX engine, which became the Apple Silicon backend in 0.19 and
  which the `-mlx` tags on `gemma4` and `qwen3.5` now expose for these
  model families. Ollama's own figures show roughly double the decode rate.
  On a project whose documented warm translation is 2.1 s, this is the
  largest single latency lever on the list.
- `qwen3.5:9b` as a draft-slot candidate against `qwen2.5:7b-instruct`.
- Qwen3-ASR against `whisper-large-v3-turbo`, carried over from last month
  with a better-specified harness.

Nothing in the testable categories produced a candidate that beats the
incumbent, and two candidates were rejected on measurement rather than on
taste. Details are in the per-category sections.

## What this sandbox could and could not do

Measured here, with the repo's own fixtures and test suite running
unmodified: the serving stack at locked and at current versions, the
`httpx` to `httpx2` migration, language detection across four backends,
the Silero 6.2.2 and 6.2.3 weight files, and the `huggingface_hub` 2.x API
surface that both ASR loaders depend on. The language-detection harness
reproduces the incumbent's recorded 99.0% and 97.8% exactly, which is the
check that it is wired up correctly rather than measuring something else.

Not testable here, and not guessed at: anything through MLX, Ollama, or the
private recordings. That covers all of the translation-model work and most
of the ASR work. Those carry a runnable spec instead of a number.

Two specific limits worth naming. First, the `httpx2` result comes from a
suite that mocks every transport, so it proves the API surface is
compatible and proves nothing about the wire. The two real-network uses,
the Silero download and the live Ollama calls, are not covered. Second, the
sandbox proxy returns 403 for raw GitHub reads, so the Silero download path
could not be exercised end to end; the weight check below is a comparison
of upstream wheel contents instead, which is stronger evidence anyway.

Dependencies were installed only into throwaway venvs under the scratchpad,
and the `httpx2` swap was applied to a copy of the repo, not to the
checkout. `pyproject.toml`, `uv.lock`, and application code were not
touched, and this PR contains only this report.

## Constraints applied

A candidate had to be free and open to use, runnable locally on Apple
Silicon and fully offline, privacy-preserving, free of Java or other heavy
runtimes, and a plausible improvement on the incumbent in accuracy,
latency, footprint, or maintenance. Two candidates failed on these grounds
and are written up rather than silently dropped: fastText `lid.176` fails
the offline rule by fetching its model from a CDN at first use, and Cohere
Transcribe has no confirmed Apple Silicon runtime. The Java rule is still
why no grammar or spelling tool is proposed, and why LanguageTool stays an
opt-in extra behind `ALLKLARO_LT=1`. Cloud-only and API-gated models were
rejected on the offline and privacy rules without further evaluation.

## Translation models

**Current:** Ollama, main model `gemma3:12b`, draft model
`qwen2.5:7b-instruct` (`server.py:421`, `static/app.js`).

Nothing in this category is testable in this sandbox. There is no Ollama,
no Apple Silicon, no GPU, and no access to the correction history or the
recordings the project's probe suite scores against. No numbers are
offered, and the research below is license, availability, and vendor claims
only.

### Gemma 4

**Candidate: `gemma4:12b`.** Apache-2.0. The Gemma 4 family launched
2026-04-02 as the first Gemmaverse release under an OSI-approved license,
and `gemma4:12b` was announced 2026-06-03 as the first mid-sized Gemma with
native audio input. Google describes it as encoder-free multimodal, with
vision and audio flowing directly into the LLM backbone, performing "nearing
our larger 26B MoE model on standard benchmarks" at less than half the
memory, and running locally on laptops with 16 GB. The Ollama library lists
`gemma4:12b` at 7.7 GB with a 256K context, against `gemma3:12b` at 8.2 GB
with a 128K context, so the candidate is slightly smaller with twice the
context.

This matters more than a normal version bump for three reasons. The license
moves from source-available to Apache-2.0. The size tier is identical, so
the RAM tier table in `README.md` does not need reworking to try it. And
the incumbent was chosen not for translation quality but because it was the
only model that followed saved corrections and dialect steering, which is a
property of instruction-following rather than of raw translation, and
exactly the sort of thing a generation bump can change in either direction.

**Recommendation: NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK, highest priority.** Spec for the
Mac:

```
ollama pull gemma4:12b
RUN_INTEGRATION=1 ALLKLARO_MODEL=gemma4:12b uv run pytest -m integration -v
uv run python tools/replay.py --audio ~/.cache/allklaro/<recording>.m4a
uv run python tools/trace_report.py
```

Metrics and baselines, from the 2026-07-27 table in `README.md`, measured
on an M4 Max with 48 GB: `gemma3:12b` scores **22/22** at **2.1 s** warm
translation and keeps every learner feature. A candidate has to reach 22/22
to displace it as main, because the probes a weaker model loses (Berlin
flavor, correction steering, gender overrides) are the features rather than
the polish. Record warm translation latency too: at equal probe scores the
faster model wins, and at 22/22 with a latency win this is a clear adopt.

Two cautions. `server.py:2158` special-cases model names containing
`qwen3`, `deepseek-r1`, or `gpt-oss` to disable thinking mode; `gemma4` is
not in that list, and the Ollama library tags `gemma4` as a thinking model.
Check whether it emits reasoning into the translation output before reading
a low probe score as a quality result. Second, `gemma4:12b` accepts audio
natively. That is out of scope for a translation benchmark and should not be
mixed into it, but it is worth knowing it exists.

### Qwen

**Candidate: `qwen3.5:9b` for the draft slot.** The Qwen 3.5 family is in
the Ollama library at 0.8b, 2b, 4b, 9b, 27b, 35b, and 122b, all at 256K
context, with the 9b at 6.6 GB to 7.6 GB. The family claims expanded
support to 201 languages and dialects. The library's reported WMT24++ score
of 78.9 averaged over 55 languages under XCOMET-XXL is a translation
benchmark and therefore the right kind of signal, but it is reported for
Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, not for the 9b, so it says nothing directly about the
tag that would actually be pulled. Qwen3.6 and Qwen3.8 also exist, at 27b
and above, which is above the draft slot's size budget.

**Recommendation: NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK, medium priority.** The draft bar
is lower and clearer than the main bar: beat `qwen2.5:7b-instruct`'s 1.5 s
without falling below its 18/22.

```
ollama pull qwen3.5:9b
RUN_INTEGRATION=1 ALLKLARO_DRAFT=qwen3.5:9b uv run pytest -m integration -v
```

One compatibility note that is good news: `server.py:2158` matches on the
substring `qwen3`, which catches `qwen3.5` and `qwen3.8` as well, so
thinking mode is already disabled for these without a code change.

### Ollama's MLX engine

**Candidate: the runtime rather than the model.** Ollama 0.19, released
2026-03-30, moved the Apple Silicon backend to MLX to use unified memory.
Ollama's own published figures, measured 2026-03-29 against 0.18 as
baseline, are 1810 tokens/s prefill and 112 tokens/s decode under 0.19 with
MLX, against 1154 and 58 under 0.18. Ollama states plainly that the two
runs used different quantization formats, so that is not a like-for-like
comparison, and roughly doubled decode should be read as an upper bound
rather than an expectation. The preview required more than 32 GB of unified
memory and initially supported only Qwen3.5. The `-mlx` tags now published
for `gemma4` (e2b, e4b, 12b, 26b, 31b) and `qwen3.5` are the evidence that
the supported architecture list has since grown to cover both families this
project would care about.

**Recommendation: NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK, high priority.** This is the
largest latency lever found this month, and it is the one that does not
require accepting a different model's behavior to collect. The 48 GB M4 Max
in the README clears the 32 GB requirement. Run the probe suite on the
MLX-tagged build of whichever model wins the comparison above, and compare
warm translation latency against the same model's default tag:

```
ollama --version                       # confirm >= 0.19
ollama pull gemma4:12b-mlx
RUN_INTEGRATION=1 ALLKLARO_MODEL=gemma4:12b-mlx uv run pytest -m integration -v
uv run python tools/replay.py --audio ~/.cache/allklaro/<recording>.m4a
uv run python tools/trace_report.py
```

Metric and baseline: warm translation latency against the same model's
non-MLX tag measured in the same session, and the probe score against
22/22. A quantization change can move output quality, so the probe score is
not a formality here; take the latency win only if the score holds.

### What was not reconsidered

`translategemma` is already evaluated and rejected in `README.md` as a
strict-translator fine-tune that ignores saved corrections and style
steering. The library now also lists a 27b size, which does not change that
reasoning. `qwen2.5:32b-instruct` keeps its documented place as the
high-RAM main model.

## Speech recognition

**Current:** `mlx-whisper` 0.4.3 (MIT), model
`mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo` (`server.py:396`).

**Maintenance check:** 0.4.3 is still the newest release on PyPI, last
uploaded 2025-08-29, unchanged since last month. On the model side,
`mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo` was last modified 2026-04-12 and
there is no newer Whisper generation. Both the package and the model are at
the end of their line, which is stability rather than neglect, but it does
mean the only way forward in this category is a different model family.

**Candidate: Qwen3-ASR, carried over from last month.** Apache-2.0, 0.6B
and 1.7B, 30 languages including German and Spanish, through `mlx-qwen3-asr`
0.4.4 (Apache-2.0, 2026-09-20). The relevant published comparison is the
Qwen3-ASR technical report's 12-language FLEURS average: **4.90 for
Qwen3-ASR-1.7B against 5.27 for Whisper large-v3**. Two caveats on reading
that. It is the vendor's own number, and it compares against large-v3 rather
than against large-v3-turbo, which is the actual incumbent and is the weaker
of the two, so the real gap could be larger or could be differently shaped.
Neither German nor Spanish is broken out in that average.

The practical story has improved since last month, which is the reason to
re-raise it rather than just restate it. The project now publishes
pre-quantized weights at 517 MB (0.6B 4-bit), 801 MB (0.6B 8-bit), 1.2 GB
(1.7B 4-bit), and 2.0 GB (1.7B 8-bit), with roughly 3.4 GB of memory for
the 1.7B at fp16, and it exposes a `Session` object for repeated calls,
which is the shape AllKlaro needs since it decodes many short chunks rather
than one long file. `mlx-audio` (MIT) also lists
`mlx-community/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-8bit`, so there are two independent runtimes.

**Recommendation: NEEDS LOCAL BENCHMARK.** License, offline story, runtime,
and language coverage all pass; only accuracy on this app's audio is
unverified, and it is unverifiable here.

```
uv pip install mlx-qwen3-asr            # into a scratch venv, not the project
uv run python tools/dump_transcripts.py --audio ~/.cache/allklaro/<recording>.wav \
    --out /tmp/whisper_baseline.jsonl
# then the same recording through Qwen3-ASR-1.7B-8bit, and:
uv run python tools/boundary_quality.py --dump /tmp/whisper_baseline.jsonl
```

Metric and baseline: transcription quality on the same 54-minute recording
that produced the existing figures, against `whisper-large-v3-turbo`. The
decisive cases are the dialect ones already catalogued in
`tools/dialect_mishearings.py` and `dialects.txt`, not aggregate WER,
because a model that wins on WER while losing "nett verstarne" style
mishearing recovery is a downgrade for this app. Also record warm per-chunk
latency against the budget documented in `PROGRESS.md`. If Qwen3-ASR wins
on both, re-run `RUN_INTEGRATION=1 uv run pytest -m integration -v` before
believing it.

**Cohere Transcribe: WATCH.** `cohere-transcribe-03-2026`, 2B, Apache-2.0,
14 languages including German and Spanish, and reported at the top of the
open ASR leaderboard. It fails the Apple Silicon rule as things stand: the
`mlx-audio` README lists Whisper, Distil-Whisper, Qwen3-ASR, Parakeet,
Nemotron, Voxtral, and others, and does not list Cohere Transcribe, so there
is no confirmed MLX path today. Two further notes against it for this app
specifically: the model card states it does no language detection, which
AllKlaro relies on, and it is documented as prone to hallucinating text on
pure silence, which is a real hazard for a pipeline that feeds it
VAD-closed chunks. Worth revisiting only if an MLX port appears.

## Fast partial ASR

**Current:** `parakeet-mlx` 0.5.2 (Apache-2.0), model
`mlx-community/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` (CC-BY-4.0, `server.py:1356`).

**Upgrade available:** `parakeet-mlx` 0.5.3 shipped 2026-10-01, the first
release since 0.5.2 in June. Upstream publishes no release notes and no
GitHub releases, so the sdists were diffed directly. The whole change is
three things: `_force_monotonic` is applied to the return values of
`merge_longest_contiguous` and `merge_longest_common_subsequence` in
`alignment.py`, fixing non-monotonic token timestamps after a merge;
`transcribe()` now raises `ValueError` for audio shorter than one hop
length instead of failing obscurely; and a README typo. Both fixes are
real, and **neither is on AllKlaro's code path.** `transcribe_partial`
calls `model.generate(mel)` on a rolling window and never touches the merge
helpers or the path-based `transcribe()`.

**Recommendation: ADOPT, as maintenance with no expected behavior change.**
It cannot be imported in this sandbox, so this is a source diff rather than
a test result. The honest case for taking it is staying close to upstream
on a package that releases rarely, not a benefit to this app. Verify with
`uv run pytest tests/test_partial_asr.py` and then
`RUN_INTEGRATION=1 uv run pytest -m integration -v` on the Mac.

**Model check:** there is no Parakeet v4. `mlx-community/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3`
is the newest in the line, last modified 2025-08-16, and NVIDIA's own
`parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` was last modified 2026-08-05. The incumbent is
current.

**Candidate rejected on evidence: `nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b`.**
NVIDIA, OpenMDW-1.1, 0.6B, cache-aware FastConformer-RNNT with configurable
80 ms to 1120 ms chunks, 40 locales with German and Spanish both in the
transcription-ready tier. Structurally this is the right shape for a partial
pass: it reuses cached encoder context and processes only new audio, where
AllKlaro re-decodes a rolling window from scratch. But NVIDIA publishes
FLEURS WER for both models on the same benchmark, and the incumbent wins
clearly:

| model | German (FLEURS WER) | Spanish (FLEURS WER) |
|---|---|---|
| `parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3` (incumbent) | **5.04%** | **3.45%** |
| `nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b` at 1120 ms | 8.31% | 4.11% |
| `nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b` at 80 ms | 9.81% | 4.87% |

German is this app's primary language and the gap there is the widest. What
the streaming model buys instead is concurrency: NVIDIA reports roughly 17x
more simultaneous streams at 80 ms chunks on one H100. That is a
multi-tenant serving concern and has no value to a single-user local app.
The card also lists no Apple Silicon or MLX support, though `mlx-audio` does
reference an `mlx-community` conversion.

**Recommendation: NO CHANGE.** Paying 3.3 points of German WER for
concurrency this app will never use is the wrong trade, and the latency it
would improve is not the constraint: a partial decode measured 71 ms warm,
against finals on the Whisper thread.

One observation that is not a tooling change and is recorded only because
the 0.5.3 diff surfaced it. The merge helpers that 0.5.3 fixes exist because
`parakeet-mlx` supports incremental decoding with cached context, and
AllKlaro does not use it, re-decoding the full rolling window every 2 s
instead. The redundancy is already cheap at 71 ms, so this is not a
recommendation, just a note that the headroom is inside the current
dependency if the partial pass ever needs it.

## Voice activity detection

**Current:** Silero VAD v6.2.2 ONNX weights under `onnxruntime` 1.27.0,
pinned by tag and verified by checksum since `b97e92c`
(`server.py:474-476`), with an energy-based fallback.

Last month's ADOPT landed and is in place:

```
SILERO_URL = ("https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad/raw/v6.2.2/"
              "src/silero_vad/data/silero_vad.onnx")
SILERO_SHA256 = "1a153a22f4509e292a94e67d6f9b85e8deb25b4988682b7e174c65279d8788e3"
```

**Upstream moved, and it does not matter.** `silero-vad` 6.2.3 shipped
2026-09-23, six days after last month's scan. Both the 6.2.2 and 6.2.3
wheels were pulled from PyPI and every bundled model file hashed. All seven
are byte-identical across the two releases, including the one this project
uses:

| file | 6.2.2 SHA-256 (first 8) | 6.2.3 SHA-256 (first 8) |
|---|---|---|
| `silero_vad.onnx` (in use) | `1a153a22` | `1a153a22` |
| `silero_vad.jit` | `e1122837` | `e1122837` |
| `silero_vad_16k_op15.onnx` | `7ed98ddb` | `7ed98ddb` |
| `silero_vad_16k_sequence.onnx` | `9ccdacc4` | `9ccdacc4` |
| `silero_vad_half.onnx` | `1e0b195a` | `1e0b195a` |
| `silero_vad_op18_ifless.onnx` | `7671cd04` | `7671cd04` |
| `silero_vad_openvino_16k.onnx` | `7776b81a` | `7776b81a` |

`1a153a22` matches `SILERO_SHA256` exactly, so the pin is still the current
weights and the 6.2.3 release carries no model change to migrate to. This
also demonstrates the pin working as intended: a release landed upstream,
and because the weights are addressed by hash rather than by branch, the
project can establish in one command that nothing changed underneath it.

**Recommendation: NO CHANGE.** No weight migration, no re-tuning of the
hysteresis thresholds (0.35 continuing, 0.5 starting), and no reason to
bump the tag. The local-benchmark spec in last month's report remains the
right procedure if a future release ever does change the weights.

`onnxruntime` 1.31.0 is covered under the serving stack below.

## Language detection

**Current:** `py3langid` 0.3.0 (BSD-3-Clause), pinned `<0.4` since
`b97e92c`, restricted to the active language pair and combined with a
lexical vote (`server.py:2846-2866`).

Last month's ADOPT landed: `pyproject.toml` now reads
`py3langid>=0.3.0,<0.4`, and `py3langid` is still at 0.4.0 upstream, so the
pin continues to do its job.

**Tested here** against `tests/fixtures/detect_phrases.py` (101 phrases and
23 traps, expanded by `cases()` to 202 and 46 across every pair containing
the phrase's language) through the server's own `detect_language`. Only
`_langid_vote` was replaced per backend; the lexical vote and the
confidence arbitration around it stay exactly as the app ships them. Each
backend ran in its own process, so the memory figures are comparable.

| backend | PHRASES | TRAPS | latency | cold start | peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| py3langid 0.3.0 (incumbent) | 99.0% | **97.8%** | 85.3 us | 0.06 s | 94 MB |
| lingua 2.2.0, full accuracy | **100.0%** | 95.7% | 69.8 us | 0.01 s | 84 MB |
| lingua 2.2.0, low accuracy | 97.5% | **97.8%** | 67.7 us | 0.01 s | 85 MB |
| fastText lid.176, uncompressed | 99.5% | 93.5% | 52.0 us | 0.65 s | 203 MB |
| fastText lid.176, compressed | **100.0%** | 95.7% | 47.3 us | 0.05 s | 73 MB |

The incumbent row reproduces the 99.0% and 97.8% recorded in
`tests/test_detect.py` and in last month's report, which is the check that
the harness measures what it claims to. Peak RSS is whole-process and
includes importing `server.py`, so the rows are comparable to each other
but are not the cost of the detector alone.

**New candidate this month: fastText `lid.176` via `fasttext-langdetect`
1.1.1.** The package is MIT; the models are CC BY-SA 3.0, which is the same
license family as the Wiktionary data the project already uses, so the
license is not the problem. Accuracy is interesting: the compressed model
ties lingua's perfect `PHRASES` score with the lightest footprint and the
fastest classification in the table.

**It is rejected on the offline rule, and the rejection is observed rather
than inferred.** On first use the library logged:

```
INFO Downloading fastText model lid.176.ftz from
https://dl.fbaipublicfiles.com/fasttext/supervised-models/lid.176.ftz
INFO Downloaded fastText model to /tmp/fasttext-langdetect/lid.176.ftz
```

That is a runtime fetch from a Meta CDN into a world-writable temporary
directory, with no revision pin and no checksum, triggered by the first
language detection rather than by an install step. For an offline-first
privacy-preserving app this is disqualifying on its own, and it is
precisely the class of problem the project just finished fixing for Silero.
It would also be a silent cross-session dependency on `/tmp` surviving.
Both fastText rows also lose on `TRAPS`, the adversarial half the fixture's
own docstring says exists to stop the word-list vote being weighted higher:
2.1 points for the compressed model and 4.3 for the uncompressed one.

**lingua: WATCH, unchanged.** Still 2.2.0, still dated 2026-03-09, so
nothing has moved since last month's assessment. The trade is the same one:
a perfect `PHRASES` score and a 10 MB lighter process, paid for with 2.1
points on `TRAPS`. The low-accuracy mode matches the incumbent's `TRAPS`
score but gives up 1.5 points on `PHRASES`, and its misses are the exact
cases the fixture was built for ("Merry Christmas!", "Cheers", "Na klar"),
so it is the worse of lingua's two configurations for this app despite the
better-looking trap number.

**Recommendation: NO CHANGE.** The incumbent is pinned, accurate, light,
fully offline, and has the best trap robustness in the table. No candidate
is better on the axis that matters, and the one that is fastest and
lightest cannot be used at all.

## Word frequency

**Current:** `wordfreq` 3.1.1 (Apache-2.0).

3.1.1 remains the newest release, still dated 2023-11-21. As recorded last
month, this is a deliberate sunset rather than neglect: the maintainer
stopped updating the data because generative AI polluted the open web
corpus it drew on and Reddit closed its public archives.

Last month identified the real risk as packaging rather than data, namely a
frozen package breaking against a future Python or numpy. **That risk was
tested this month and did not materialize:** the full suite passes with
`wordfreq` 3.1.1 under numpy 2.5.4 on Python 3.12, so the package has
survived two numpy minor releases since it was last touched.

No freely available, offline, comparable-coverage replacement surfaced.

**Recommendation: NO CHANGE.** The sunset is arguably a feature here.
`zipf_frequency` is used to judge whether a word is common enough to be
worth surfacing to a learner, and a pre-2021 human-language snapshot is a
better instrument for that than a corpus contaminated with model output.

|---|---|---|
| German | 98,333,861 bytes | 2026-10-03 |

Last month's operational note therefore still stands and is now a month

**New finding: `build_noun_forms.py` pulls from a moving branch.**
`CSV_URL` at `build_noun_forms.py:27` is:

```
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gambolputty/german-nouns/main/german_nouns/nouns.csv
```

This is the same reproducibility problem the project just fixed for Silero,
in the same shape: a core data file fetched from a branch rather than a
revision, so two builds from the same commit of AllKlaro can produce
different `de_noun_forms.tsv` files, with nothing in the tree recording
which. The compiled output feeds gender and plural notes injected into
translation prompts, so a silent change upstream is a silent change to model
behavior.

The upstream repository publishes no tags and no releases, so there is no
version to pin to. The realistic fix is the same pattern already proven in
`load_silero()`: put a commit SHA in the URL in place of `main`, and verify
a recorded SHA-256 of the CSV after download.

**Recommendation: ADOPT the pin** for `build_noun_forms.py`. **NO CHANGE**
otherwise; no alternative source offers better coverage under a license
this permissive and a workflow this offline, and no FreeDict release news
surfaced, which is normal for that project's cadence.

### httpx to httpx2

**This resolves last month's WATCH item, and it is larger than it looked.**
The deprecation warning the last report flagged
(`Using 'httpx' with 'starlette.testclient' is deprecated; install 'httpx2'
instead`) is not a test-only curiosity. `httpx2` 2.13.1 (BSD-3-Clause,
2026-09-23) is the continuation of `httpx` under new stewardship by Pydantic
Services Inc., with Tom Christie still credited as author, created because
`httpx` had seen limited recent activity. The incumbent `httpx` 0.28.1 was
last released 2024-12-06, which is 22 months of no releases on the HTTP
client that talks to Ollama.

**Tested here.** AllKlaro's `httpx` surface is small: `httpx.AsyncClient`
and `httpx.ConnectError` in `server.py`, one `httpx.get` for the Silero
download, and `httpx.MockTransport` plus `httpx.Response` in the tests.
Five files import it, one line each. The swap was applied to a copy of the
repo under the scratchpad, changing `import httpx` to
`import httpx2 as httpx` and nothing else.

| arm | result |
|---|---|
| current stack, core deps only, TestClient and app on `httpx` 0.28.1 | 764 passed, 36 skipped, 2 deselected, 19.48 s |
| current stack, core deps only, TestClient and app on `httpx2` 2.13.1 | **764 passed, 36 skipped, 2 deselected**, 20.49 s |

The two deselected tests are the `test_speakers.py` embedding tests that
need torch, which the core-only venv does not have; they are deselected in
both arms, and they pass in the `speakers` runs above. The `httpx` arm emits
the deprecation warning and the `httpx2` arm emits none.

Separately, in the `speakers` venv, `httpx2` arrived on its own as a
transitive dependency, and Starlette's `TestClient` picked it up
automatically: all 767 tests passed with the test client running on
`httpx2` rather than on `httpx`. So the migration is verified twice, once
deliberately and once by accident.

**Recommendation: ADOPT.** Five one-line import changes, no API
differences encountered, a maintained client in place of one that has been
still for 22 months, and the deprecation warning goes away for free.

**Be candid about what this does not cover.** The suite mocks every
transport, so this proves API compatibility and proves nothing about the
wire. Two real-network legs are untested: the live Ollama calls, which need
the Mac, and the Silero download, which could not be exercised because the
sandbox proxy returns 403 for raw GitHub. On that second one, both clients
accepted the exact call `server.py:1538` makes,
`get(url, follow_redirects=True, timeout=60)`, and both returned a
`Response` with `.status_code` and `.content`, so the signature is
confirmed even though the download is not. Gate the change on:

```
RUN_INTEGRATION=1 uv run pytest -m integration -v
rm ~/.cache/allklaro/silero_vad.onnx   # force the download path once
uv run python -c "import server; server.load_silero()"
```

The second command is the one that matters, because the checksum in
`load_silero()` will catch a truncated or redirected download rather than
letting it pass silently.

### huggingface-hub 1.x to 2.x

**WATCH, and it is new since last month.** `uv.lock` pins
`huggingface-hub` 1.24.0. Upstream released 2.0.0 on 2026-09-24, five days
after the last scan, and is at 2.2.0 as of 2026-10-08. Both `mlx-whisper`
and `parakeet-mlx` depend on it with no version constraint, so the next
`uv lock` refresh crosses a major version on the package that fetches the
weights for both ASR models. It also pulls `httpx2` in transitively,
because `huggingface_hub` 2.2.0 requires `httpx2<3,>=2.0.0`, which is how
it appeared unbidden in the test run above.

**Partially de-risked here.** The two call sites were read out of the
packages and checked against `huggingface_hub` 2.2.0 installed in this
sandbox:

| package | call | status in 2.2.0 |
|---|---|---|
| `mlx-whisper` 0.4.3 | `snapshot_download(repo_id=...)` | present, `repo_id` accepted |
| `parakeet-mlx` 0.5.3 | `hf_hub_download(id, filename, cache_dir=...)` | present, all three accepted |

Both functions still exist in 2.2.0 and still accept exactly the arguments
these packages pass, so the jump is likely to be uneventful. That is a
static check of two signatures, not a download, so it lowers the risk
rather than clearing it. Whoever next refreshes the lockfile should delete
one cached model and confirm it re-downloads before trusting it.

## Candor about these findings

The best finding this month is a correction, not a discovery. Last month's
report asserted that Gemma 4 did not exist, and it had existed for five
months at the time, with the 12B size, the one that matters for this
project, available for three. That is the second substantive error in two
reports from this series, and both have the same cause: stating a negative
about the outside world from inside a sandbox that cannot see it, instead
of checking. A scan that cannot run the thing it reports on should be read
with that in mind, and the per-category sections above try to make the line
between "measured here" and "read on a model card" impossible to miss.

What is solid: the Silero hash comparison is a fact about files, and the
seven-way byte-identity across 6.2.2 and 6.2.3 settles that category
completely. The language-detection numbers run the project's own fixture
through the project's own code path and reproduce the project's own
recorded baseline, and the fastText rejection rests on a log line this run
produced rather than on a reading of documentation. The serving stack and
`httpx2` results are the repo's own suite, green, twice each, and their
blind spot is named: every transport is mocked, so nothing there touches a
real socket.

What is weakest: everything in the translation and ASR sections. Not one
number in them was measured here, and the FLEURS figures that drive the
Nemotron rejection are read speech from a vendor's own card, while this app
runs conversational German with Hessisch and Worms dialect across three or
more speakers. The Nemotron recommendation is still the confident one in
that group, because the comparison is between two models from the same
vendor on the same benchmark and the gap on German is 3.3 points, which is
wide enough to survive a lot of benchmark mismatch. The Gemma 4 and Ollama
MLX items are the opposite: they are the highest-value items on the list
and carry no evidence about this app at all, only a license, a size, and a
vendor's throughput claim that the vendor itself labels as not
like-for-like.

Everything handed to the Mac is handed over because it could not be
measured here, not because it was measured and came out ambiguous.
